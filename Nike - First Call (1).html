Nike priorities.

01 · Win now Rebuilding the fundamentals.

A year-old turnaround focused on rebuilding Nike's fundamentals with a leaner organization.

02 · One foundation One connected technology foundation.

Nike consolidated supply chain, operations, and technology under one new COO to integrate everything end-to-end.

03 · The marketplace An integrated marketplace.

Rebuilding wholesale partnerships to blend direct and retail sales into one consumer-led marketplace.

What's driving Nike right now

01

The "Sport Offense" reorg is the strategic bet to reverse it.

↻

Nike restructured roughly 8,000 employees into vertical sport teams, each obsessing over a single consumer group. Running and the World Cup football push are proving the model early, and Spring '27 will be the first full season of product built end-to-end by these teams.

02

Performance sports and the World Cup are the current bright spot.

↻

Running has grown double digits for five straight quarters and gained market share; the World Cup activation drove 1.5 billion views and the fastest-ever 24-hour cleat launch — real evidence the Sport Offense approach can work.

03

Nike is deliberately trading revenue for margin quality.

↻

It cut off-price business by more than 50%, pulled $2B of classic-franchise inventory out of the market, and is tightening buys — all pointed at a stated path back to double-digit EBIT margins, even though it hurts near-term top line.

04

Tech investment is shifting from consumer-facing to end-to-end operations.

↻

Nike explicitly redeployed resources from its Direct/digital tech teams toward planning, supply chain, and manufacturing — the clearest signal in the call that its next technology priority is internal operations, not the storefront.

Finding the use case

01Store allocationRight product, right Nike-owned door
01

Getting the right product to the right Nike-owned door, in the channel Nike fully controls.

Today
  • Nike owns the data & process end to end on Nike.com and stores
  • Store-level allocation still performs poorly, by Nike's assessment
  • No consistent product hierarchy vs. last season
Opportunity
  • Door-level allocation recs, refreshed continuously
  • Prove lift in the one channel nothing blocks
  • Working model in weeks, benchmarked vs. what's in flight

Status: an internal AI allocation effort exists; scope & timing unknown. Ben's framing — moving fast enough, could it go further?

02WholesaleUntapped data for smarter selling
02

Using the partner data Nike already has to sell in better and help partners sell through.

Today
  • nike.net moves ~$11B in partner transactions
  • Data arrives 3 ways: EDI, Excel, in-platform ordering
  • Partner POS in a separate repo, path back unclear
  • Sell-in insight work manual; AI pitch-deck pilot unscaled
Opportunity
  • Turn partner data into account/door-level sell-in recs
  • Accelerate the existing pilot instead of rebuilding
  • Start in one geography, prove it, then scale
  • Phase 2: push insight to partners via the portal

Status: Ben offered the intro to the sell-in team. Scope is one geography, not global.

Retail Elevation

Retail Elevation Today: Fragmented and Manual.

Elliot Hill is asking: "How are you tracking this weekly? How are you adapting for next week?"

The answer needs to be there before continuing to scale the investment.

The tension underneath

What's standardized from corporate, and what makes each market unique?

The same activation lands differently everywhere — some markets have passion points nothing global covers.

ExampleOne launch, three reactions

A basketball shoe launch tied to Caitlin Clark resonates differently in LA, Indiana, and New York.

ExampleLocal passion, no global campaign

Boxing and basketball matter more in markets like the Philippines than in most of Nike's global calendar — but nothing is built globally to capture that.

Why that's hard to answer today

01Brand Spend

Owned by brand teams. Covers activations, imagery, in-store storytelling. Not tied to sales performance data.

02Sales Spend

Owned by sales/wholesale teams. Covers fixtures, signage, door-level investment. Tracked separately from brand.

03POS Data

Structured, but sits in its own system. No link to what activation or spend drove the result.

04Store Reports

Manual, inconsistent across doors and cities. No standard format to compare markets.

05Activation Assets

Captured ad hoc. Useful for storytelling, not for measuring impact.
